Jörmungandr

A particle accelerator the size of the Earth.

In Norse myth, Jörmungandr is the sea serpent that grew until it circled the world and bit its own tail. This one is a vacuum tube 40,030 km long, floating 200 m under the oceans, steered by 2.1 million magnets. Light needs 133.5 ms to get round it.

Enrico Fermi sketched the same machine in 1954: a ring around the planet reaching 5 PeV, to be finished in 1994 for $170 billion. This page runs the numbers again. Everything below is computed live from the formulas at the bottom.

The route

No circle around the Earth misses land completely. Testing every possible ring against the Natural Earth coastline map, the one that crosses the least land is tilted 66° to the equator. It runs from Tasmania across the whole Pacific to Alaska, through northern Canada, down the Atlantic to West Africa and round the Southern Ocean back to Tasmania.

That leaves 3,451 km of tunnel to bore, 8.6% of the ring. A ring along the equator would need 8,762 km. Pick a crossing to turn the globe to it.

    A ring over both poles would cross only 2,651 km of land, but it has to pass under the Antarctic ice sheet at 83° S and under two floating ice shelves, so it is ruled out.

    Under water, not on pylons

    A tube 50 m up in the air fails three ways.

    • The ocean is 3,700 m deep on average. The pylons would be towers four times taller than any building, standing in hurricanes.
    • Steel in open air swings about 30 °C between seasons. Over 40,030 km that is 14 km of growing and shrinking.
    • The beam throws off gamma rays, and a lost beam sprays particles. In air nothing stops them.

    So the tube floats 200 m down, held by tethers to the seabed, and goes into bored tunnel where it meets land.

    • Storm waves fade with depth. At 200 m their motion is down to 1.5%, and every ship passes far overhead.
    • The pressure is 21 bar, routine for a submarine hull.
    • 200 m of water is 240 nuclear interaction lengths, about the shielding the LHC gets from 100 m of rock.
    • The hardest radiation to stop is muons, which fly about 25 km through water at these energies. Leaving the tube in a straight line, they stay under water for 50 km before the curved surface drops away.

    The maths

    • Magnet limit: E = Z e c B ρ, with bending radius ρ = 0.8 × 6,371 km, because magnets fill 80% of the ring.
    • Energy radiated per lap: U = Z²e²γ⁴ / 3ε₀ρ.
    • Energy gained per lap: Z e × 30 MV/m × the length of cavities.
    • Top energy: the magnet limit, or 99% of the energy where loss equals gain, whichever is lower.
    • Laps to get there: integrate dE/dn = gain − U, which gives n = (Eeq / gain) × ½(artanh x + arctan x) with x = E / Eeq.
    • Typical photon energy: 3ħcγ³ / 2ρ.
    • Beam: 0.74 A, the LHC's bunches (1.15 × 10¹¹ protons every 25 ns) all the way round.
    • Muons decay with lifetime γ × 2.2 µs, counted over every lap of the ramp.
